# Zero Trust Architecture


## chunk_id
- 9.1

## category
- security_architecture

## type
- security_pattern

## definition
- a security model that assumes no implicit trust, verifies every access request, and continuously enforces least privilege.

## when_to_use
- internet-facing systems and multi-tenant platforms
- microservices where east-west traffic needs strong identity
- environments with frequent change and dynamic infrastructure

## when_not_to_use
- small isolated systems where overhead would exceed risk reduction
- when identity and policy enforcement cannot be operated reliably

## decision_signals
- need: internet-facing systems and multi-tenant platforms
- need: microservices where east-west traffic needs strong identity
- willing to accept: adds latency and complexity due to pervasive authentication and authorization

## advantages
- reduces blast radius by removing network-based trust assumptions
- improves auditability through explicit identity and policy checks

## disadvantages
- adds latency and complexity due to pervasive authentication and authorization
- requires strong identity, key management, and policy governance

## anti_patterns
- using this when small isolated systems where overhead would exceed risk reduction
- use this when identity and policy enforcement cannot be operated reliably
- ignoring the downside that adds latency and complexity due to pervasive authentication and authorization

## real_world_examples
- enforcing mTLS between services and validating workload identity on every call
- requiring device posture and MFA for administrative access
