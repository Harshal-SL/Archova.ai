# High Availability vs Fault Tolerance


## chunk_id
- 2.9

## category
- architecture_decisions

## type
- decision

## definition
- a reliability tradeoff between minimizing downtime (high availability) and continuing correctly through failures (fault tolerance).

## when_to_use
- high availability for user-facing services with strict uptime slos
- fault tolerance for safety-critical or revenue-critical workflows
- high availability for multi-az and multi-region deployments

## when_not_to_use
- fault tolerance for early-stage systems where cost is prohibitive
- high availability without considering data consistency and recovery

## decision_signals
- need: high availability for user-facing services with strict uptime slos
- need: fault tolerance for safety-critical or revenue-critical workflows
- willing to accept: both require higher cost and operational complexity

## advantages
- high availability reduces outages via redundancy and failover
- fault tolerance prevents incorrect outcomes during partial failures

## disadvantages
- both require higher cost and operational complexity
- fault tolerance increases coordination and testing burden

## anti_patterns
- using this when fault tolerance for early-stage systems where cost is prohibitive
- using this when high availability without considering data consistency and recovery
- ignoring the downside that both require higher cost and operational complexity

## real_world_examples
- multi-az web tier with automated failover (ha)
- replicated consensus-based control planes (ft)
