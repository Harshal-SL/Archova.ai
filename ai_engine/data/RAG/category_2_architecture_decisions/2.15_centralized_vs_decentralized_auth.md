# Centralized vs Decentralized Auth


## chunk_id
- 2.15

## category
- architecture_decisions

## type
- decision

## definition
- an identity and access design choice between a central authority and distributed enforcement across services.

## when_to_use
- centralized identity for consistent policies and auditability
- decentralized enforcement for low-latency service-to-service checks
- centralized auth at the edge with shared token validation

## when_not_to_use
- decentralized auth when policy drift and duplication are unacceptable
- centralized auth when it becomes a single point of failure

## decision_signals
- need: centralized identity for consistent policies and auditability
- need: decentralized enforcement for low-latency service-to-service checks
- willing to accept: centralized auth can become a bottleneck under load

## advantages
- centralized auth simplifies governance and compliance
- decentralized checks reduce coupling and improve availability

## disadvantages
- centralized auth can become a bottleneck under load
- decentralized auth increases maintenance and coordination

## anti_patterns
- using this when decentralized auth when policy drift and duplication are unacceptable
- using this when centralized auth when it becomes a single point of failure
- ignoring the downside that centralized auth can become a bottleneck under load

## real_world_examples
- oauth2 with a shared identity provider
- service-to-service mtls with distributed authorization
