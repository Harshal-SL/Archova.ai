# Read-Heavy vs Write-Heavy Design


## chunk_id
- 2.5

## category
- architecture_decisions

## type
- decision

## definition
- a workload-driven design choice to optimize primarily for reads or primarily for writes.

## when_to_use
- read-heavy systems to justify caching and read replicas
- write-heavy systems to justify batching and partitioning
- mixed workloads to separate read and write models with cqrs

## when_not_to_use
- before measuring actual traffic and access patterns
- when added complexity exceeds the performance benefit

## decision_signals
- need: read-heavy systems to justify caching and read replicas
- need: write-heavy systems to justify batching and partitioning
- willing to accept: wrong assumptions can lock in poor architecture

## advantages
- improves performance and cost by focusing on bottlenecks
- guides indexing, caching, and storage choices

## disadvantages
- wrong assumptions can lock in poor architecture
- optimizations often increase operational complexity

## anti_patterns
- using this when before measuring actual traffic and access patterns
- use this when added complexity exceeds the performance benefit
- ignoring the downside that wrong assumptions can lock in poor architecture

## real_world_examples
- social timelines are read-heavy at scale
- telemetry ingestion pipelines are write-heavy
