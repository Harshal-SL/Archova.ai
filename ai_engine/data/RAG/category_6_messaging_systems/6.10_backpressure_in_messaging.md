# Backpressure In Messaging

## chunk_id
- 6.10


## category
- messaging_systems

## type
- messaging_pattern

## definition
- controlling intake and processing rates so consumers do not get overwhelmed and queues do not grow without bound.

## when_to_use
- bursty producers and slower consumers
- preventing memory pressure and timeouts in consumers
- protecting downstream dependencies during partial outages

## when_not_to_use
- when the workload must be accepted at all costs and buffering is guaranteed
- when the system lacks levers to throttle producers or shed load

## decision_signals
- need: bursty producers and slower consumers
- need: preventing memory pressure and timeouts in consumers
- willing to accept: can increase latency and reduce throughput during throttling

## advantages
- stabilizes the system under overload
- reduces cascading failures across services

## disadvantages
- can increase latency and reduce throughput during throttling
- requires coordinated controls across producer, broker, and consumer

## anti_patterns
- use this when the workload must be accepted at all costs and buffering is guaranteed
- use this when the system lacks levers to throttle producers or shed load
- ignoring the downside that can increase latency and reduce throughput during throttling

## real_world_examples
- limiting consumer concurrency based on database saturation signals
- pausing consumption when lag exceeds a threshold to trigger load shedding upstream
