# Delivery Semantics

## chunk_id
- 6.5


## category
- messaging_systems

## type
- messaging_pattern

## definition
- the guarantees about how many times a message may be delivered: at-most-once, at-least-once, or exactly-once.

## when_to_use
- selecting broker and consumer behavior for reliability needs
- designing retries and deduplication for side effects
- evaluating tradeoffs between latency, cost, and correctness

## when_not_to_use
- when the team cannot implement idempotency and observability
- when exactly-once is assumed without verifying end-to-end guarantees

## decision_signals
- need: selecting broker and consumer behavior for reliability needs
- need: designing retries and deduplication for side effects
- willing to accept: stronger semantics often reduce throughput or increase complexity

## advantages
- makes failure behavior predictable and designable
- enables consistent operational expectations for retries

## disadvantages
- stronger semantics often reduce throughput or increase complexity
- exactly-once is hard across network boundaries and external systems

## anti_patterns
- use this when the team cannot implement idempotency and observability
- use this when exactly-once is assumed without verifying end-to-end guarantees
- ignoring the downside that stronger semantics often reduce throughput or increase complexity

## real_world_examples
- at-least-once event delivery with idempotent consumers for order events
- at-most-once best-effort notifications where duplicates are worse than drops
