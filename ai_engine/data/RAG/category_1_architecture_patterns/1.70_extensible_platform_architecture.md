# Extensible Platform Architecture


## chunk_id
- 1.70

## category
- architecture_patterns

## type
- architecture

## definition
- an architecture that provides shared capabilities as a platform and enables teams or partners to build extensions on top through stable apis.

## when_to_use
- multiple products or teams needing shared identity, billing, and observability
- building an ecosystem with third-party integrations and extensions
- standardizing governance and developer experience across many services

## when_not_to_use
- early-stage products without repeated platform needs
- when platform governance would slow down core product iteration

## decision_signals
- need: multiple products or teams needing shared identity, billing, and observability
- need: building an ecosystem with third-party integrations and extensions
- willing to accept: platform work can become a bottleneck and requires strong product ownership

## advantages
- accelerates delivery by centralizing common capabilities
- improves consistency in security, compliance, and operations

## disadvantages
- platform work can become a bottleneck and requires strong product ownership
- risk of over-abstraction and premature generalization

## anti_patterns
- using this when early-stage products without repeated platform needs
- use this when platform governance would slow down core product iteration
- ignoring the downside that platform work can become a bottleneck and requires strong product ownership

## real_world_examples
- internal developer platforms providing templates and shared services
- marketplace platforms exposing stable apis for partners
