# Backup, Restore, and Point-in-Time Recovery


## chunk_id
- 5.24

## category
- database_design

## type
- data_pattern

## definition
- planning backup schedules and restore procedures so data can be recovered to a known point with tested rpo and rto.

## when_to_use
- protecting against accidental deletes, corruption, and ransomware
- meeting business continuity requirements
- supporting disaster recovery drills and audits

## when_not_to_use
- when the data is fully derivable from other immutable sources and rebuild is acceptable
- when backups are impossible due to encryption key loss or unsupported tooling

## decision_signals
- need: protecting against accidental deletes, corruption, and ransomware
- need: meeting business continuity requirements
- willing to accept: backups incur storage costs and operational overhead

## advantages
- reduces data loss risk and shortens recovery time
- enables controlled restores for testing and forensics

## disadvantages
- backups incur storage costs and operational overhead
- restores can be slow and error-prone without frequent drills

## anti_patterns
- use this when the data is fully derivable from other immutable sources and rebuild is acceptable
- use this when backups are impossible due to encryption key loss or unsupported tooling
- ignoring the downside that backups incur storage costs and operational overhead

## real_world_examples
- nightly full backups with continuous logs for point-in-time restore
- regular restore drills into a staging environment to validate procedures
