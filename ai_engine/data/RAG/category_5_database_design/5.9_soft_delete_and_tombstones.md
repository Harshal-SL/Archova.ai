# Soft Delete and Tombstones


## chunk_id
- 5.9

## category
- database_design

## type
- data_pattern

## definition
- representing deletions as state transitions (deleted_at, status) to support auditability, replication safety, and undo.

## when_to_use
- needing recovery windows or legal hold requirements
- preventing resurrection bugs in replicated or eventually consistent systems
- supporting asynchronous cleanup and retention policies

## when_not_to_use
- when regulations require hard deletes immediately
- when deleted data must never be queryable even with access controls

## decision_signals
- need: needing recovery windows or legal hold requirements
- need: preventing resurrection bugs in replicated or eventually consistent systems
- willing to accept: complicates queries and indexing (must filter deleted)

## advantages
- supports audit trails and easy recovery
- reduces race conditions when deletes propagate asynchronously

## disadvantages
- complicates queries and indexing (must filter deleted)
- can increase storage and require cleanup jobs

## anti_patterns
- use this when regulations require hard deletes immediately
- use this when deleted data must never be queryable even with access controls
- ignoring the downside that complicates queries and indexing (must filter deleted)

## real_world_examples
- marking accounts as deleted and purging after a retention period
- using tombstones in event streams to signal removals to downstream caches
